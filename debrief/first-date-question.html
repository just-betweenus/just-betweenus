The First-Date Question

September 2026 · Dating · Personal Investigation

Being in my almost mid-twenties, the looming reality that I'm still single is very much a thing.

Of course, I want to find someone. But I also want to have fun. And honestly, I don't think those two things have to cancel each other out.

I'm not sitting here desperately searching for a boyfriend. If anything, I think what I want is a not-boyfriend boyfriend. Something easy. Something seamless. The kind of thing where you're just hanging out, seeing each other, doing life together, and eventually it's like... wait. We're basically together.

But unfortunately, dating apps come with their own little issue.

You can put "long-term relationship" on your profile. You can only swipe on people who say they're looking for the same thing. You can carefully vet who you're matching with.

And you still have absolutely no idea whether the guy you're meeting actually wants to go on a date with you... or just wants to get in your pants.

It's not anxiety. It's annoyance.

Especially on Tinder.

Because that's the annoying part. I'm not scared to sleep with someone on the first date, and I'm not clutching my pearls over the possibility of having sex with a man I met on an app.

I'm just tired of not being able to filter out someone's actual intentions until I'm already sitting across from them catching a vibe.

Because sometimes you genuinely don't know.

Maybe he's taking you out because he likes you. Maybe he's interested in getting to know you. Maybe he thinks you're funny and pretty and actually wants to see where this goes.

Or maybe dinner and drinks are simply step one of his plan to get you back to his apartment.

And the frustrating thing is, those can look exactly the same at first.

At this age, I do find myself thinking more about fucking on the first date than I did before.

Am I giving the wrong impression? Is he going to talk to me after? Did he actually like me, or was this literally the entire objective?

Which is funny because in college, I really didn't think about it that much.

I hooked up with guys. I dated guys. Sometimes I fucked them. But I usually knew them already, and more importantly, I wasn't sitting there looking for something serious.

Sometimes I was simply looking to get off.

Very different circumstances.

Now, there's this weird middle ground.

I want to be free. I want to have fun. And if I meet someone and we have chemistry and I want to sleep with him, I don't necessarily want to spend the entire night calculating whether that's going to ruin my chances of ever hearing from him again.

But it also feels unfair that I have to walk into a date already wondering if this guy is planning to screw and dump me.

And honestly, if that's what you want, that's fine.

Go have sex. Pick someone up at a bar. Find someone on an app who is also just looking to hook up. There are plenty of people who want exactly that.

But why are we pretending to go on a date?

Why are we matching when my profile says I'm looking for something long-term? Why are you asking me to get drinks? Why are we spending each other's Tuesday night together if your entire intention was just getting me into bed?

That's really the part that gets me.

I don't care if someone just wants sex. I care about wasting my time pretending we're looking for the same thing when we're not.

Maybe I'm reading too much into it. Maybe the secret is actually just sleeping with him and seeing what happens.

Maybe sleeping with someone on the first date doesn't tell you anything about whether they'll stick around. Maybe waiting doesn't either.

I honestly don't know.

But I do know that I would like to go on a date without having to wonder whether I'm actually on a date.

But that's just between us.
