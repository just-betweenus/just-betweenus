Was I Wrong to Cancel a Date Because He Was Short?

August 2026 · Dating · Personal Investigation

There are certain moments in online dating where you have to stop and ask yourself: am I being picky, or am I just saving us both some time?

This was one of those moments.

I met this guy on Tinder, and we talked for about a week before we had plans to meet. Honestly, things were going pretty well. He had even sent me $20 for a drink before we'd gone on the date, which I thought was funny. I told him I was out drinking when I absolutely was not, and apparently he believed me enough to fund the imaginary cocktail.

The day of the date, we'd already confirmed everything when he texted me, "I think I can find her insta."

I said, "?"

He replied, "texting my friend about you lol."

Okay. Interesting.

So I gave him my Instagram, we followed each other, and I did what any normal, well-adjusted woman would do: immediately investigated the situation.

And that's when things took a turn.

I started scrolling through his Instagram and noticed something. Every single picture seemed to feature him surrounded by absolutely massive men. Not just tall men. Huge men. And he was noticeably smaller than all of them.

Like... significantly smaller.

At first I thought maybe I was imagining it. So naturally, I did more digging.

And then I found an old baseball profile.

Five foot nine.

Now, I understand that to some people, this is completely normal. To me, this was a crisis.

My absolute shortest is usually 5'10". I don't know how this man made it past my internal screening process, but somehow he did. I had apparently failed to conduct the necessary due diligence.

And suddenly I was sitting there thinking, Oh my God. No.

The worst part was that I had already been mentally preparing for the date. We had plans. We'd talked for a week. There was nothing objectively wrong with him. And yet, once I knew his height, I was completely icked out.

So I did what I always do when I don't know if I'm being rational: I brought it to the group chat.

The responses were split almost exactly down the middle.

Half said, "Go. You might like him."

The other half basically said, "If you're already turned off, don't waste your time."

And I kept going back and forth because I knew exactly what the argument was going to be.

You're really going to cancel a date because a guy is 5'9"?

And, well...

Yes.

I canceled about four hours before the date. I told him something had come up and I wasn't going to be able to make it.

He said, "aww okay."

And that was it.

I never heard from him again.

And honestly? I don't feel that bad about it.

Maybe that's the part people won't agree with.

But I don't know this man. We had never met. We had never gone on a date. There was no relationship to ruin and no commitment I was breaking. I had already realized that I wasn't attracted to him in the way I wanted to be, and I don't think I owe someone a first date simply because they seem nice on paper.

That's the weird thing about dating apps. We're all pretending we're looking for some deeper, more meaningful connection, but the entire process begins with looking at a series of photographs and deciding whether you find a stranger attractive enough to potentially meet.

We swipe left for someone's haircut. Their clothes. Their smile. Their facial hair. Their entire vibe.

But somehow height is where we're supposed to suddenly become morally principled?

I don't really buy that.

Physical preferences are physical preferences. I'm allowed to want a man who is taller than me. I'm allowed to want to wear heels and still feel like I'm standing next to someone who makes me feel small. I'm allowed to have a type.

And yes, maybe it's shallow.

I can admit that.

But I also think there's a difference between being shallow and being honest.

If I already know that 5'9" is going to bother me, why would I go on the date and pretend it doesn't? Why would I sit across from him for two hours knowing that I'm already trying to convince myself to be attracted to him?

That feels worse.

Because then I'm wasting his time too.

Maybe he would have been hilarious. Maybe we would have had incredible chemistry. Maybe I would have completely forgotten about his height once we met.

Or maybe I would have spent the entire date thinking about how short he was and wondering how quickly I could get home.

I'll never know.

And I'm okay with that.

I think people who haven't spent much time on dating apps don't always understand how much of dating online is about making decisions with very little information. You're constantly deciding whether someone is worth a conversation, whether the conversation is worth a date, whether the date is worth a second date, and whether the second date is worth potentially letting this person into your life.

There is no perfect way to do it.

Sometimes you give someone the benefit of the doubt.

Sometimes you don't.

And I don't think every person deserves a trial period just because you might theoretically be compatible.

Maybe he would have been the love of my life. Maybe he would have been the most boring man I've ever met. Maybe he was actually 5'10" and the baseball profile was wrong.

We'll never know.

And I'm okay with that.

What I do know is that I saw the 5'9", looked at the giant-man Instagram archive, consulted the council, and decided I wasn't getting in that Uber.

Maybe it was shallow. Maybe it was reasonable. Either way, I wasn't getting in that Uber. And that's between us.
